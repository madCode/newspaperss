# Naming: a clean-room persona panel

*4 Oct 2026.* A fresh panel, with no access to the code, the screenshots or the app's current
name, was given only what the app does and the problem it solves, and asked to brainstorm names
as the five people in [personas.md](personas.md) (a Kindle reader in their sixties, a Pocket
refugee with a Kobo, a self-hoster with tt-rss and KOReader, a Boox owner, a TalkBack user) plus
a naming critic who isn't a user. The name stays newspapeRSS for now; this is input for later.

Clash checks were a few web searches, not a trademark search.

## Ranked top 5

1. **Doorstep.** The paper is waiting when you wake: delivered, bounded, calm. One common word
   with one spelling, so it reads cleanly in TalkBack, and it names no technology, so it covers
   sites, saved links and a self-hosted server alike. No reader-app clash found. Tagline idea:
   "Your sites, delivered to your e-reader. Then it ends."
2. **Paper Route.** About delivery from start to finish; friendly, two easy words. Less ownable
   than Doorstep, and it names the carrier rather than the reading.
3. **Endpaper.** The most bookish and distinctive: the last sheet of a book, which fits a paper
   that ends. Loved by the e-reader enthusiasts; the Kindle reader needed it explained.
4. **Last Page.** The plainest "it ends" of any name, but a common phrase is hard to find in a
   store. Perhaps better as a tagline, or as the name of the end page.
5. **Morning Fold.** Beautiful and papery, but it ties the app to mornings when the time is the
   reader's choice, and sits close to an existing "Daily Fold" trademark.

**Honorable mentions:** Slowpaper (right ethos, but screen readers can break it and "slow" sounds
like a complaint), Front Porch (crowded by home and real-estate brands), Daybook (journaling apps
use it a lot).

## What each person wanted

- **The Kindle reader:** "a newspaper that turns up and then stops. If it sounds like a gadget,
  I won't trust it." Doorstep, The Morning Paper, Paper Route, Front Porch, Daybreak.
- **The Pocket refugee:** "my saved links end up somewhere and stay put." Satchel, Paper Route,
  Keepsake, Tote, Endpaper.
- **The self-hoster:** "don't call it Feed-anything; it should sound like a press." Broadsheet,
  Quire, Press Run, Folio, Slowpaper.
- **The Boox owner:** "it should feel like paper, not a send-to button." Endpaper, Morning Fold,
  Inkwell, Stillpaper, Last Page.
- **The TalkBack user:** "I hear the name before anything else. One obvious spelling, no
  capital-letter tricks, and it shouldn't sound like another word." Doorstep, Paper Route, Last
  Page, Morning Paper. Warned off Quire (heard as "choir") and Slowpaper.
- **The naming critic:** "you can't own a phrase. Pick one concrete image: a place, an object, a
  ritual. The best here is the paper landing on the step. Avoid 'news' or 'feed': it puts you
  back in the noise."

## The panel on the top 5

- **Doorstep:** "That's how I want my news" (Kindle reader); "One clear word. Perfect" (TalkBack
  user); "The one to beat" (critic).
- **Paper Route:** "Delivery is the whole point" (Pocket refugee); some voices say "root", some
  "rout", but people know it either way (TalkBack user).
- **Endpaper:** "What's an endpaper?" (Kindle reader); "My favourite" (Boox owner); "needs a
  tagline" (critic).
- **Last Page:** "Says the philosophy outright" (Boox owner); "Hard to search for" (self-hoster);
  "a great tagline, a weaker brand" (critic).
- **Morning Fold:** "Lovely" (Kindle reader); "But I read it at night" (Pocket refugee).

## Names to avoid, and why

- **Paperboy:** taken several times over, including an RSS-to-Kindle service
  ([paperboy.it.com](https://paperboy.it.com/support)), a direct competitor.
- **Kindle, Kobo, Pocket, Boox, PocketBook** in the name: trademarks, and it shuts out the other
  devices.
- **"Feed", "RSS", "News", "EPUB"** in the name: technical, and signals a firehose, not calm.
- **Puns that only work in writing,** capital-letter tricks and invented spellings: TalkBack reads
  them oddly, and someone who hears the name can't spell it. (Worth weighing against the current
  name, whose pun lives in its capitals.)
- **Quire:** heard as "choir".
- **Morning Edition, Gazette, Herald, Daily anything:** existing publications (Morning Edition is
  NPR's), or too generic to find.
- **Folio, Inkwell, Broadsheet:** widely used, or an existing media brand. Also seen: ZinFeed, an
  iOS "RSS to EPUB" app.

## Round 2: calm, the paper, the e-reader, your own front page

*6 Oct 2026.* A second clean-room panel, with the same people and the same rules: no code, no current name. This
time it played on calm, the newspaper, the e-reader and owning your reading, and left the
"it ends" theme to round 1. Clash checks are web searches, not a trademark search; before choosing,
search USPTO/EUIPO and the Play Store directly.

### Ranked top 5

1. **Private Press.** The book-arts term for a one-person printing press. "Private" also says
   no account and no tracking. Two plain words. No app clash found, though searches kept
   returning PressReader, so not conclusive. Tagline: "A newspaper with a circulation of one."
   - "Private is the whole point. Nobody can shut my paper down." (Pocket refugee)
   - "A real term that fits the product exactly. Check the trademark." (critic)
2. **Hand Press.** You set the type and pull the sheet yourself, and it lands in your hand on
   the e-reader. No app clash; a small Racket package uses the name. Pick one spelling
   (two words) and keep it. Tagline: "Your paper, set by hand."
   - "Sounds like a tool, not a startup. I'd trust it." (self-hoster)
3. **Quiet Edition.** An edition of your own paper, minus the noise. The calmest name and the
   clearest aloud, but generic and buried among "Quiet" apps in store search.
   - "That's exactly what I want from the morning." (Kindle reader)
4. **Morning Proof.** A printer's proof, the first fresh sheet, read on a paper-like screen.
   No clash found. "Proof" has other meanings, and "morning" suggests a fixed time.
5. **Stillpress.** A press that runs quietly. Minor clashes (Stillpoint Digital Press,
   Stillhouse Press, the Stillwater News Press app), and one word or two is hard to guess.

### What each person wanted

- **The Kindle reader:** "the paper on the table with my coffee, not another app." Quiet
  Edition, Morning Proof, Armchair, Sunday Paper, Easy Chair.
- **The Pocket refugee:** "my things stay mine, so it can't vanish like Pocket did." Private
  Press, Keepsake, Clippings, Home Edition, Tuck.
- **The self-hoster:** "a craft term, not a startup word; I set the type myself." Hand Press,
  Private Press, Typeset, Letterpress, Masthead.
- **The Boox owner:** "ink and paper, grey and quiet." Stillpress, Newsprint, Fresh Ink,
  Inkling, Grayscale.
- **The TalkBack user:** "two ordinary words I can say and spell first time." Quiet Edition,
  Private Press, Hand Press, Armchair, Home Edition.
- **The naming critic:** "concrete, ownable, findable: one image, not a mood board." Private
  Press, Hand Press, Masthead, Morning Proof, Stillpress.
- **E-ink and paper puns** didn't survive being read aloud: "E-dition" is just "edition",
  "Printless" sounds like "pointless". None beat the plain two-word names.

### Names to avoid, and why

- **Masthead:** an iOS app that turns RSS feeds into news briefings
  ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761660344)).
- **Fresh Ink:** a calm daily-reading app on both stores
  ([reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1482668117/fresh-ink/reviews)).
- **Newsprint:** a past iPhone RSS reader ([review](https://www.macstories.net/iphone/newsprint-review/)), and it says "news".
- **Inkling:** an established e-book company.
- **Letterpress:** a well-known word game.
- **Clippings:** Clippings.io, a Kindle highlights tool, and Kindle's own "My Clippings" file.
- **Sunday Paper:** Maria Shriver's publication.
- **Home Edition:** close to The Home Edit (a brand with an app and a show), and reads like a
  software tier.
- **Armchair:** a published RSS-newspaper app concept already uses it ([post](https://death.id.au/b1.14)); generic.
- **Fine Print:** contract-reading apps, and it suggests legal small print.
- **Calm Press:** reads as the Calm app's press page.
