How they show an account's feeds

Screenshots from each product's own store or project page. Three are tt-rss (TTRSS-Reader and tt-rss itself); the rest are other synced accounts. Every one shows unread counts on every feed, the thing newspapeRSS leaves out.

What the screenshots say

  • Folding categories, feeds inside is what nearly everyone does (tt-rss, NetNewsWire, Fiery Feeds, Readrops, NewsFlash). That's F1.
  • Feeds indented under one heading (Read You) is F5, your idea: no category level between the account and its feeds.
  • Drilling in a level at a time (TTRSS-Reader) is A's account page and F4: tidy, one more tap.
  • None of them has to stay calm: they're readers, built around counts. The paper's Sources list doesn't need counts, so it can be shorter than any of these.

What the sync APIs allow

  • tt-rss: subscribe into an existing category, unsubscribe, read the tree. Nothing to make, rename or move categories (API).
  • Google Reader API (FreshRSS, Inoreader): add, and folders are tags, so it can make them (Inoreader).
  • Miniflux: creates categories; feeds and categories carry server settings, including hide_globally (API).
  • Fever: read-only; can't add or edit anything (FreshRSS).

What it means here

  • Among full feed readers, grouping an account's feeds under it is the norm (NetNewsWire, Readrops, Capy). The closest finite-edition peers (ttrss2calibre, Reabble) don't group at all: they send everything unread.
  • Paper settings belong in the app, keyed by the feed's address (Fluent Reader). tt-rss can't hold them, and an address survives signing in again or moving a feed.
  • Clutter is handled with show/hide, not more structure: Reeder's "show in Home", Miniflux's hide. That's today's "leave out".
  • Write-back is explicit: NetNewsWire asks which account and folder. Nobody found sends feeds to a server silently.
  • Digests get scoped: Inoreader by folder, KTool by daily or weekly per feed. "Too long" is the usual complaint about all-subscriptions digests (HN).
  • No one found builds a finite edition from a reader account; ttrss2calibre and Reabble send everything unread.
Cycle 2 · baseline

tt-rss today

One source for the whole account. Its feeds are already there underneath: each article remembers its feed, and the planner gives each feed its own turn and applies the per-source cap per feed. What the reader can set per feed stops at a checkbox.

What a tt-rss feed can't do that a feed added here can

  • Article text (Automatic / Feed's text / Full page), and learning it from three days of evidence.
  • Its own cap ("at most 1 from this site"): tt-rss follows the edition setting.
  • A section: every tt-rss article lands in one section of the contents.
  • A page of its own: Recent articles mixes all 58, so one busy local-news feed fills the top of the list (the paper itself already gives each feed its turn).

What works and stays at the account

  • Sign-in, "Articles from" one category, read sync with tt-rss, Start fresh.
  • Sync asks each feed for up to five unread, so per-feed sources cost no extra requests.

Built from the code on main and your screenshots. Feed names are made up.

Cycles 3–5 · v3

A. Each feed opens up, folded under the account

Cycle 16: the list of feeds now sits on Sources, inset under the tt-rss row (F5); see All feeds in Sources. Sources doesn't change: tt-rss is still one row. On the account page, "Feeds in your paper" becomes the list of feeds, folded by tt-rss category, each opening a page of its own.

What the reader gets

  • Article text learned per feed, with no new screen. This alone fixes tt-rss's teaser and stripped feeds.
  • A page per feed: Leave out or Bring back, an Article text override, a cap, a section, its own recent (or starred) articles.
  • Categories folded, saying what's inside in words. With one category under "Articles from", the rest fold into "Not in your paper", and only their waiting articles expire.
  • A feed unsubscribed in tt-rss says so ("No longer in your tt-rss") and offers Forget; its stars still go in.
  • Changes made while an edition is being made apply from the next one, and the dialog says so.

How it's kept (round 2 changed this)

  • A feed is a settings row, not a source. One row per tt-rss feed (account, feed id, address, category, article text, cap, section, left out), replacing today's left-out table. Articles stay on the account.
  • Why: the planner already keys turns by account + feed. Sync, read sync and mark-read are all keyed on the account and stay untouched. Making feeds into sources would rewrite exactly those.
  • Content, caps and sections look up the article's feed row before the account's.
  • The full feed list (quiet feeds included, with address and category) is read once a day.
  • Signing in again: the same user at a new address (http to https) keeps the rows; another user clears them, as today.

For

  • Sources stays four rows for everyone.
  • Leaves sync and delivery alone.
  • Every later per-feed idea (a heart, how often) has a home.

Against

  • Two levels to learn: the account, and a feed.
  • A lookup in three places (text, caps, sections).
  • A per-feed cap rarely bites in a 30-minute paper; balance needs D.

Round 2 · cycle 5 · the code and the edges

  • Code: child sources would break every account-keyed path: read sync, mark-read, the kind = 'TTRSS' joins, and Sources and OPML export would gain 58 rows. Reduced: per-feed settings table keyed like the planner's publicationOf; articles stay on the account.
  • Code: text learning, caps and sections all read the account today (recordFullText returns early, caps skip tt-rss). Fixed: one lookup by the article's feed.
  • Self-hoster: there's no address to carry settings by: the app asks only for feeds with unread, without their URL or category, and an http→https change deletes the account. Fixed: a daily full feed list; the rows are re-keyed rather than deleted.
  • Owner: choosing a category still expires every waiting article. Fixed: only those outside it.
  • Owner: a feed unsubscribed on the server looks merely quiet. New: "No longer in your tt-rss", with Forget.
  • Owner: a left-out feed's page hid its starred articles. Fixed: they're listed.
  • Owner: Leave out mid-build still lands in today's paper. Fixed: "from the next edition".
  • TalkBack: a heading role on a button hid the button. Fixed: a button inside a heading, with a live expanded state.

Cycles 6–8 · dropped in round 2

B. Sources by section

Folded into D. v1 spread all 58 tt-rss feeds through Sources; round 1 cut it to an optional by-section view; round 2 found that view duplicated D's section pages and tripped too easily. What survives lives in D: a section's page lists its phone feeds and its tt-rss feeds as one row. The phone below is v2, kept for the record.

What it taught

  • "Sources reads like the contents" is a real wish, met by D's section page.
  • Every row needs its freshness line, tt-rss feeds included.
  • Left-out feeds belong out of the way, not among live ones.

Why it went

  • Sections exist for almost everyone (the reading list is always "Saved for later"; one OPML folder adds more), so the view would switch on by surprise.
  • A second place to browse sections, with its own toggle.
  • The filtered account page hid every account setting.

Round 2 · cycle 8 · verdict: fold into D

  • Kindle reader in their sixties: the reading list always has a section and an OPML import adds folders, so "two sections" trips by surprise: Sources reorganises overnight under a friend's folder names.
  • Owner: with categories-as-sections off, Sources showed a heading "Tiny Tiny RSS" over one row "Tiny Tiny RSS · 54 feeds".
  • Owner: the filtered account page had no way to the account's settings, and its menu offered "Remove tt-rss" from inside a section.
  • Owner: two Uncategorized feeds had no group at all and couldn't be found.
  • TalkBack: five rows all named "Tiny Tiny RSS · N feeds".
  • Everyone: D's section page already does this job. Dropped; the useful part moved to D.

Round 1 · cycle 7 · personas

  • Kindle reader, no tt-rss: B forces sections on five starter feeds that have none. Reduced: headings only with two or more sections.
  • Owner: with "Articles from: News", Sources still listed 49 feeds that never reach the paper, and left-out feeds sat among live ones. Fixed: only feeds in the paper are listed; left out folds at the bottom.
  • Owner: tt-rss rows lost the freshness line that's there to catch dead feeds. Fixed.
  • TalkBack, Boox: about 67 swipe stops; "News 10" heard as an unread count; full refresh on every fold. Fixed: tt-rss folded per section, headings read "News, 3 sources and 9 tt-rss feeds, folded".

Cycles 9–11 · v3

C. Add to tt-rss instead

Built on A. When a feed is found, the usual Add keeps it on the phone, one tap as today. With an account connected, a second button, Add to tt-rss instead, subscribes it there. Move to tt-rss waits.

What happens

  • Already in your tt-rss? Checked against every tt-rss feed by address (quiet ones too). If it's there, the app opens it instead.
  • Category starts on the paper's own; others warn if the paper takes one category.
  • "Keep only its newest 2 unread", ticked. Done at the first sync that sees the feed, by article, because tt-rss fetches on its own schedule: marking right away would mark nothing, and "everything" would starve the paper.
  • Undo only for a feed this app just created (tt-rss's "subscribed" answer, never "already subscribed"). It unsubscribes, drops anything it brought here, and offers the phone.
  • Timeout or Cancel after asking is "waiting for tt-rss", kept on the account page across restarts, settled at the next sync.
  • Several feeds on one site: the same chooser as the phone add. Substack and similar: says what tt-rss loses, on this path only.
  • Untouched: onboarding, starter packs and OPML stay phone-only, skipping feeds already in tt-rss.

Cost

  • A stored "waiting for tt-rss" state: a database change with a migration test.
  • The feed's id comes from tt-rss's answer where newer servers give it, otherwise from the full feed list by address.

For

  • One subscription list across your tt-rss apps, when you want it.
  • No extra step for the common add.
  • Undo, so a wrong tap isn't a laptop chore.

Against

  • tt-rss strips some content: a phone feed can be better.
  • A site may block the server but not the phone.
  • More reliance on a project now living on a fork.

Round 2 · cycle 11 · the API and the code

  • API: subscribing doesn't fetch; tt-rss's updater does, later. Marking back issues read at once marks nothing, and "sync now" brings nothing. Fixed: trimmed at the first sync that sees the feed; the message says "usually within the hour".
  • Code: the paper takes only unread, five per feed. Marking all read would starve it. Fixed: keep the newest 2, mark the rest by id.
  • API: subscribe returns a status, not reliably an id; "already subscribed" must never offer Undo. Fixed.
  • Code: today's feed list asks only for feeds with unread, without addresses, so "already in tt-rss" would miss quiet feeds. Fixed: the full list, matched by cleaned-up address.
  • Code: Cancel after the request is sent can leave a subscription the app forgets. Fixed: treated as a timeout, stored.
  • Code: Undo left waiting articles that read sync can't resolve. Fixed: they're dropped.
  • Owner: a dialog on every add slows the common case. Reduced: a second button instead.

Round 1 · cycle 10 · personas

  • Owner on one category: a feed added to another category never reaches the paper. Fixed: category starts on the paper's, with a warning.
  • Owner: subscribing dumps the feed's backlog unread into the laptop counts. Fixed: "Mark its older posts read in tt-rss", on by default.
  • Owner, self-hoster: a remembered "tt-rss too" plus no way to unsubscribe makes a wrong tap a laptop chore. Fixed: phone is the default; Undo unsubscribes.
  • OPML, starter packs: importing your own tt-rss OPML makes 58 duplicates. Fixed: imports skip feeds already in tt-rss; they never ask.
  • Slow server: a timeout then "Add to this phone" makes a duplicate once the server finishes. Fixed: a timeout is "not sure yet", checked at the next sync.
  • Owner: Move to tt-rss can orphan settings if tt-rss stores a different address, or drop stars before tt-rss has fetched anything. Reduced: Move is deferred.
  • Self-hoster: Substack through tt-rss loses Notes and footnotes. Fixed: the dialog says so for known hosts and keeps the phone preselected.
Rounds D1–D2 · v3

D. Balance the paper by section

With 54 feeds and seven slots, per-feed controls don't shape the paper. What the owner wants is "thoughtful, varied, some fun": every section represented, and more of some. One rule the reader can predict:

Each section gets one article. Then More sections take the next turns, then Normal. Less gets a second only if there's room. Starred articles always go in, and count as their section's one.

What changes

  • Settings › Sections, shown only with two or more real sections. Off by default: importing a friend's OPML never changes the paper by itself.
  • Less / Normal / More per section, applied per section, not per source: a 21-feed section doesn't win just by being big.
  • Only with Take turns. With In order or Shuffle, the screen says balance steps aside.
  • Within a section, sources still take turns, featured-longest-ago first; so does the order sections get their one article, so a section cut today leads tomorrow.
  • "What would go in now" on request, each heading saying its share and count ("Long reads · More · 2") and why.
  • When only one section has news, the contents say so ("Only Front page had new articles today") rather than padding or holding back.
  • Saved for later is a section: with balance on, a saved link goes in most days. The screen says so.
  • Sections come from OPML folders today; for tt-rss, A's "categories as sections".

For

  • "Varied" by construction: every section, when the budget allows.
  • A rule you can say out loud and check against the paper.
  • Works for OPML folders now, tt-rss after A.

Against

  • Long articles in the first round can still fill the budget.
  • One more screen in Settings.
  • Changes how often saved links appear.

Round 2 · D2 · does it deliver?

  • Owner, worked numbers: today's turns already give Long reads about 3 of 7 articles (21 of the 47 sources in the worked example). Weighting per section, More gave about 2.5: fewer essays than with balance off. Per source, Science and Saved vanished. Fixed: a guaranteed one per section, then More.
  • Owner: two 20-minute essays taking the first turns fill the paper; "More" meant less variety. Fixed: everyone gets one before anyone gets two.
  • Everyone: "weights whose turn comes next" can't be predicted. Reduced: one rule, said on the screen, in the preview and in the contents.
  • Kindle reader: a friend's OPML with 12 folders switched balance on, giving a 1-feed folder as many turns as a 40-feed one. Fixed: off by default.
  • Shuffle and In order: weights are unpredictable or do nothing there. Fixed: Take turns only, and said.
  • Owner: only Front page (Less) had news, and the paper was all news with no word why. New: a line in the contents.
  • Stars, Saved for later: does a starred essay use Long reads' turn? Fixed: yes, it's the section's one. Saved links become near-daily. Said on the screen.

Round 1 · D1 · the planner and the personas

  • Pocket refugee: stars go first across every source; a section's minutes would hold back starred saved links. Fixed: stars ignore shares (refined in D2: a star counts as its section's one).
  • Owner: length is only known after fetching, so a budget per section means going over once per section, and a 25-minute essay alone breaks "Long reads 12". Fixed: one budget; shares only weight the turns.
  • Everyone: minutes that must add up to "30 of 30" break when the size changes, and 0 clashes with Leave out. Reduced: Less / Normal / More.
  • Owner: leftover time went to whichever section was held back first. Fixed: the second pass follows the shares too.
  • Code: the contents order comes from Sources' order; dragging would make a second owner. Reduced: no dragging; no "Make a section".
  • Boox: an honest preview means fetching pages. Fixed: on request, lengths "about".
  • Kindle reader, TalkBack: steppers and a drag handle with no names; a screen she'd never need. Fixed: hidden under two sections; labelled choices.
Your question · what would sections of a newspaper look like?

Sections in the paper

A section is a heading the paper's articles are grouped under: Front page, Long reads, Science. Today they come from OPML folders, and the reading list is always "Saved for later". tt-rss has none yet: all of it lands in one section. Below, three ways the book itself could show them, on an e-reader page. Tap the contents entries and the page edges to turn pages.

S1 · today

Headings in the contents, the section in each kicker

  • Contents ("In this edition") lists articles under small uppercase headings, each with its minutes.
  • Each article starts with a kicker: "Long reads · A Working Library".
  • No section pages: turning from the last Science article lands straight on the first Arts one; the kicker is the only sign.
  • Kindle's Go To menu lists sections and their articles.

Cheapest, already built. Works well when sections are few and short.

S4 · recommended · S1, plus two signposts

Today's book, with the section said where it changes

  • A section flag on the first article of each section: the section's name, large, above the kicker. No extra page, so no extra page turn or e-ink refresh.
  • "Next, in Science: Tagging shorebirds at dawn · 4 min" when the next article starts a new section, on the "Next" line that already follows long reads.
  • Contents, kicker and Go To stay as today: Kindle opens at the contents, and each section's entry already points at its first article.

The cheapest way to make sections felt while reading. Try the section's first article, then the end of a long read.

S2 · section fronts · only for big sections

A short page opens each section

  • The section's name, large, its article count and minutes, and its headlines with one line each: a pause and a menu, as a print section's front does.
  • Contents gets shorter: sections with their minutes; tap one for its front.
  • Kicker keeps only the source, since the front says where you are.
  • Costs one page per section: five page turns in a 30-minute paper. On e-ink, each is a full refresh.

Feels most like a newspaper, but in a 7-article paper most fronts would open a one-article section and repeat its headline a page later. If kept: a front only for a section with 3 or more articles, and the section stays in the kicker for readers who jump in from Go To.

S3 · a front page · not recommended

One page with the lead of every section

  • The first page after the cover is a front page: each section's lead headline, its first two lines, and "Long reads · 2 articles · 19 min →".
  • Then the articles, with section headings in the contents as today (S1).
  • The cover image already shows first headlines, so this is a richer version of something that exists.
  • Fitting five leads on a small e-reader screen means short teasers; on a 6" Kindle at large type, it scrolls to a second page.

Kindle opens the book at the contents, after this page, so it would be skipped; KOReader and Boox open at the cover, which already lists six headlines. A third list of the same leads.

Critique · personas on a Kindle, a Boox and KOReader

  • Owner, Kindle: S3's front page sits before the contents, where Kindle opens the book, so it's skipped; the cover already lists six headlines. Not recommended.
  • Boox: S2 adds five page turns, each a full refresh, and three of its fronts open one-article sections. Reduced: fronts only for sections of 3+, section kept in the kicker.
  • Owner: S1's real gap is the silent change between sections. New: S4, a section flag and a section-aware "Next", at no page cost.

Where the sections themselves would come from

  • OPML folders (today): a folder's name becomes the section.
  • tt-rss categories (the "categories as sections" switch from A): Essays & ideas, News, Tech… as they're named in tt-rss.
  • The reading list is always "Saved for later".
  • Renamed or merged here for a newspaper's voice: News → "Front page", Essays and Newsletters → "Long reads". That's a small extra: a name per section, kept in the app.
  • No section (starter feeds): no heading, as today. A paper with one section shows no headings at all.
Your question · what if every tt-rss feed showed on the Sources page?

All feeds in Sources

Three layouts that put all 58 tt-rss feeds on Sources, built with what B's critiques taught: every row keeps its freshness line, left-out feeds go out of the way, the account's settings keep a home, and a reader without tt-rss sees exactly today's list. None needs feeds to become sources in the database: the per-feed table from A1 can supply the rows.

F1 · by account

"On this phone" and "Tiny Tiny RSS", as NetNewsWire does it

  • Two groups: your phone's sources, then tt-rss with its feeds under their tt-rss categories.
  • The tt-rss heading is a row: tap it for the account (sign-in, Articles from, read sync, Start fresh).
  • Categories fold; all open here, so every feed shows. On TalkBack that's about 70 stops, and on e-ink every fold redraws the list.
  • Freshness from tt-rss itself ("newest in tt-rss: today"): the app only fetches unread, so its own "last new article" would call a feed you read on the laptop dead.
  • Left out folds at the end of the tt-rss group.

For

  • Matches how tt-rss itself and most readers show an account.
  • Clear where a feed lives and where its settings are.

Against

  • Your three phone sources sit above 58 rows; the reading list is easy to scroll past.
  • Says nothing about the paper's sections.
F2 · by the paper's sections · not recommended

Sources laid out like the paper's contents

  • Grouped by section, in the paper's order: Front page, Long reads, Science… Each lists its phone feeds and its tt-rss feeds together; tt-rss ones carry a small "tt-rss" label.
  • The account is one row at the top, "Tiny Tiny RSS · connected".
  • No section gets its own group; Left out folds at the bottom.
  • Only when you have tt-rss or two real sections; otherwise today's list.

For

  • What you see is how the paper is put together.
  • Pairs with D: each group could show its share (More, Normal, Less).

Against

  • This is B again: an OPML import with folders rearranges Sources by surprise.
  • Changing a feed's section moves its row; with sections off, it's one long group.
  • A second place deciding the paper's order, which D avoids.
F3 · one list, with filters

Everything A to Z, with a search box and filter chips

  • One alphabetical list of every source; tt-rss feeds say "From your tt-rss" under the name. A–Z puts "The Morning Wire" on the phone right next to "Morning Wire" in tt-rss: the only layout that shows duplicates.
  • Chips: All · On this phone · tt-rss · Left out. Search finds a feed by name.
  • The account is a row pinned at the top.

For

  • Fastest way to find one feed you remember by name.
  • No structure to keep in sync with tt-rss.

Against

  • A list of 61 says nothing about the paper; it reads like a feed reader's sidebar.
  • Search and chips are more to learn for a non-coder (hidden without tt-rss).
F4 · optional · the full list, one tap down

Sources keeps one tt-rss row; the account page lists all 58

  • Sources is today's, for everyone. The reader with five starter feeds never sees any of this.
  • The account page: its settings, then every feed A to Z, a search box, and chips for In your paper and Left out. Settings and feeds on one page.
  • Each row says its tt-rss category and how fresh it is in tt-rss, and opens the feed's own page.
  • A–Z shows a feed you have both here and in tt-rss, and says so.

This answers "can I see all my tt-rss feeds?" without making Sources a feed reader's sidebar. It's A's account page with a flat, searchable list instead of folded categories.

F5 · your idea · inset under the tt-rss row · v2

tt-rss is a source, and its feeds sit just beneath it

  • Sources looks as it does today down to the tt-rss row. Under it, indented a little and joined by a thin line, are its 54 feeds in the paper, A to Z.
  • The tt-rss group is always last: a feed you add later lands above it, not under 54 rows and the Add button.
  • Two clear controls on the tt-rss row: "Settings" opens the account; a round ▾ / ▴ button shows or hides the feeds.
  • Starts folded (today's Sources) until you open it; then it stays as you left it.
  • A line under a feed only when it says something: "Nothing new in 3 weeks", "Also on this phone", or its own settings. 54 rows of "newest today" would be noise.
  • Left out is one row at the end, opening a plain list of the four. Without tt-rss, nothing changes.

For

  • Every feed on Sources, no extra tap, and still plainly part of tt-rss.
  • One level: no categories to open (unlike F1).
  • Folded, it's today's screen.

Against

  • Open, Sources is ~60 rows: closer to a feed reader's sidebar than to the paper.
  • No search or categories (F4 has search; F1 has categories).
  • On TalkBack ~60 stops open; on e-ink, folding redraws the list.

Critique · F5

  • Owner: a feed added after tt-rss landed under all 54 rows, behind the Add button. Fixed: tt-rss group always last.
  • Everyone: the fold arrow was the same chevron that means "opens a page", and the account had no visible way in. Fixed: a round ▾ button and a "Settings" link.
  • TalkBack, Boox: label said 58 but showed 54 + one; open every visit. Fixed: "Show 54 feeds in your paper", starts folded.
  • Boox, TalkBack: 54 identical "newest today" lines. Reduced: a line only for quiet feeds, duplicates and own settings.
  • Owner: the same site on the phone and in tt-rss sat 30 rows apart, unlinked. Fixed: "also on this phone" / "also in your tt-rss".
  • Owner: the Left out screen repeated the account page. Fixed: a plain list.
