What the sync APIs allow

  • tt-rss: subscribe into an existing category, unsubscribe, read the tree. Nothing to make, rename or move categories (API).
  • Google Reader API (FreshRSS, Inoreader): add, and folders are tags, so it can make them (Inoreader).
  • Miniflux: creates categories; feeds and categories carry server settings, including hide_globally (API).
  • Fever: read-only; can't add or edit anything (FreshRSS).

What it means here

  • Grouped under the account is the norm (NetNewsWire, Readrops, Capy). Only the most limited clients fold an account into one item.
  • Paper settings belong in the app, keyed by the feed's address (Fluent Reader). tt-rss can't hold them, and an address survives signing in again or moving a feed.
  • Clutter is handled with show/hide, not more structure: Reeder's "show in Home", Miniflux's hide. That's today's "leave out".
  • Write-back is explicit: NetNewsWire asks which account and folder. Nobody found sends feeds to a server silently.
  • Digests get scoped: Inoreader by folder, KTool by daily or weekly per feed. "Too long" is the usual complaint about all-subscriptions digests (HN).
  • No one found builds a finite edition from a reader account; ttrss2calibre and Reabble send everything unread.
Cycle 2 · baseline

tt-rss today

One source for the whole account. Its feeds are already there underneath: each article remembers its feed, and the planner gives each feed its own turn and applies the per-source cap per feed. What the reader can set per feed stops at a checkbox.

What a tt-rss feed can't do that a feed added here can

  • Article text (Automatic / Feed's text / Full page), and learning it from three days of evidence.
  • Its own cap ("at most 1 from this site"): tt-rss follows the edition setting.
  • A section: every tt-rss article lands in one section of the contents.
  • A page of its own: Recent articles mixes all 58, so one busy local-news feed fills the top of the list.

What works and stays at the account

  • Sign-in, "Articles from" one category, read sync with tt-rss, Start fresh.
  • Sync asks each feed for up to five unread, so per-feed sources cost no extra requests.

Built from the code on main and your screenshots. Feed names are made up.

Cycle 3 · v1

A. Each feed opens up, folded under the account

Sources doesn't change: tt-rss is still one row. On the account page, "Feeds in your paper" stops being a checkbox dialog and becomes the list of feeds, each opening a page like any feed's.

What changes

  • A tt-rss feed gets a page: Leave out, Article text, its own cap, a section, and its own recent articles.
  • "Use your tt-rss categories as sections", a switch on the account page.
  • Recent articles for the whole account moves one tap down ("All recent articles"), so the account page leads with feeds.
  • Settings are kept by the feed's address, not tt-rss's id for it, so they survive signing in again and, later, moving a feed between the phone and tt-rss (the Fluent Reader pattern).

For

  • Sources stays four rows for everyone.
  • Per-feed control for the veteran, one tap deeper.
  • Every later per-feed idea (heart, schedule) has a home.

Against

  • A migration: each tt-rss feed becomes a source under the account.
  • Two levels to learn: what's set on the account, what on a feed.
  • 58 rows on one page is still long.
Cycle 6 · v1

B. Feeds spread through Sources, by section

The account stops being a source at all. Every tt-rss feed is a row in Sources beside the feeds added here, grouped by section, with tt-rss categories as the starting sections. The account is a small "connected" row with only account settings.

What changes

  • Sources is grouped by section, the same sections the paper's contents use. Sections fold.
  • A tt-rss feed is marked with a small "tt-rss" tag; otherwise it's a source like any other.
  • The account row holds sign-in, Articles from, read sync and Start fresh.

For

  • One kind of source: no "where do I set this?"
  • Sources reads like the paper's contents.
  • Feeds added here and tt-rss feeds about the same thing sit together.

Against

  • Sources goes from four rows to sixty.
  • Pausing "all of tt-rss" has no obvious home.
  • Sections become a thing everyone meets, not only tt-rss users.
Cycle 9 · v1

C. Adding feeds back to tt-rss

Built on A. With a tt-rss account connected, adding a feed asks where it should live: just for the paper, on this phone, or in your tt-rss too, in a category you pick. A feed already here can move to tt-rss, settings and all.

What changes

  • Add a source asks "Where should it live?" only when an account is connected. The last answer is remembered.
  • Move to tt-rss in a feed's menu. Its settings move with it, kept by address.
  • Only existing tt-rss categories can be picked: the API can't make new ones.
  • Not offered: unsubscribing in tt-rss from here; the reading list and curated lists (tt-rss can't read them).

For

  • One subscription list across your tt-rss apps.
  • tt-rss fetches all day; nothing rolls off a busy feed between phone syncs.
  • Explicit, as NetNewsWire does it.

Against

  • A paper-only feed becomes an unread count in tt-rss.
  • tt-rss strips some content (footnotes, Substack Notes).
  • A site may block the server but not the phone.
  • More reliance on a project that now lives on a fork.
