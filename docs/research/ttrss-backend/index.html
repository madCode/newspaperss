What the sync APIs allow

  • tt-rss: subscribe into an existing category, unsubscribe, read the tree. Nothing to make, rename or move categories (API).
  • Google Reader API (FreshRSS, Inoreader): add, and folders are tags, so it can make them (Inoreader).
  • Miniflux: creates categories; feeds and categories carry server settings, including hide_globally (API).
  • Fever: read-only; can't add or edit anything (FreshRSS).

What it means here

  • Grouped under the account is the norm (NetNewsWire, Readrops, Capy). Only the most limited clients fold an account into one item.
  • Paper settings belong in the app, keyed by the feed's address (Fluent Reader). tt-rss can't hold them, and an address survives signing in again or moving a feed.
  • Clutter is handled with show/hide, not more structure: Reeder's "show in Home", Miniflux's hide. That's today's "leave out".
  • Write-back is explicit: NetNewsWire asks which account and folder. Nobody found sends feeds to a server silently.
  • Digests get scoped: Inoreader by folder, KTool by daily or weekly per feed. "Too long" is the usual complaint about all-subscriptions digests (HN).
  • No one found builds a finite edition from a reader account; ttrss2calibre and Reabble send everything unread.
Cycle 2 · baseline

tt-rss today

One source for the whole account. Its feeds are already there underneath: each article remembers its feed, and the planner gives each feed its own turn and applies the per-source cap per feed. What the reader can set per feed stops at a checkbox.

What a tt-rss feed can't do that a feed added here can

  • Article text (Automatic / Feed's text / Full page), and learning it from three days of evidence.
  • Its own cap ("at most 1 from this site"): tt-rss follows the edition setting.
  • A section: every tt-rss article lands in one section of the contents.
  • A page of its own: Recent articles mixes all 58, so one busy local-news feed fills the top of the list.

What works and stays at the account

  • Sign-in, "Articles from" one category, read sync with tt-rss, Start fresh.
  • Sync asks each feed for up to five unread, so per-feed sources cost no extra requests.

Built from the code on main and your screenshots. Feed names are made up.

Cycles 3–5 · v2

A. Each feed opens up, folded under the account

Sources doesn't change: tt-rss is still one row. On the account page, "Feeds in your paper" stops being a checkbox dialog and becomes the list of feeds, folded by tt-rss category, each opening a page of its own.

What changes

  • Article text is learned per feed, with no new screen. The planner already knows each article's feed; the automatic check just keeps one answer per feed instead of none. This alone fixes tt-rss's teaser and stripped feeds.
  • A tt-rss feed gets a page: Leave out (or Bring back), an Article text override, a section, its own recent articles.
  • Categories start folded, each saying what's inside in words: "Tech · 10 feeds, 1 left out". Own settings are written out on the row, not shown as a dot.
  • With one category chosen under "Articles from", only its feeds are listed; the rest fold into "Not in your paper".
  • The section row says what applies now: the account's section until "Use tt-rss categories as sections" is on. Uncategorized means no section.
  • Settings belong to the feed's source. The address only carries them when the feed is found again (signing in again, moving it). A feed with settings of its own stays listed even if it's quiet for months.

For

  • Sources stays four rows for everyone.
  • Per-feed text, the change that matters most, needs no UI.
  • Every later per-feed idea (a heart, how often) has a home.

Against

  • A migration: each tt-rss feed becomes a source under the account.
  • Two levels to learn: the account, and a feed.
  • A per-feed cap rarely bites in a 30-minute paper (7 slots, 54 feeds): balance needs D.

Round 1 · cycle 4 · personas

  • Owner: the section row said "News, its tt-rss category" while the categories switch was off. Which is in charge? Fixed: the row says what applies now.
  • Owner: with "Articles from: Essays", 46 feeds still looked live. Fixed: they fold into "Not in your paper".
  • Owner: a per-feed cap of 1 changes nothing when 54 feeds share 7 slots; what's missing is "less news, more essays". New: proposal D.
  • Everyone: per-feed article text needs no screen. Reduced: learned automatically; the page only overrides.
  • Self-hoster: keying settings by address loses them when a feed moves to https or is quiet for a month. Fixed: settings live on the feed's source; the address only carries them.
  • Boox, TalkBack: 58 open rows and a 7px dot. Fixed: folded categories, settings in words, real headings and switch labels.
  • Owner: what does a left-out feed's page look like? Fixed: a "Left out" line and Bring back.
Cycles 6–8 · v2

B. Sources by section

v1 spread all 58 tt-rss feeds through Sources. Round 1 cut it down to what was worth keeping: Sources can be shown by section, the way the paper's contents read, once there are two or more sections. tt-rss stays folded: in each section it's one row, "Tiny Tiny RSS · 9 feeds".

What changes

  • A flat list until it isn't: with fewer than two sections, Sources is today's list. The Kindle reader with five starter feeds never sees a heading.
  • By section: each section lists its own feeds and, folded, the tt-rss feeds in it. Tapping the tt-rss row opens A's account page, filtered to that section.
  • Left out goes to the bottom, folded: "Left out · 4".
  • Every row keeps its freshness line, tt-rss feeds included, so dead feeds are still easy to spot.

For

  • Sources reads like the paper's contents.
  • Phone feeds and tt-rss feeds about the same thing sit together.
  • Costs nothing for readers with one section.

Against

  • Needs A underneath, and sections that mean something (D).
  • A second way to find a tt-rss feed (by section, by account).

Round 1 · cycle 7 · personas

  • Kindle reader, no tt-rss: B forces sections on five starter feeds that have none. Reduced: headings only with two or more sections.
  • Owner: with "Articles from: News", Sources still listed 49 feeds that never reach the paper, and left-out feeds sat among live ones. Fixed: only feeds in the paper are listed; left out folds at the bottom.
  • Owner: tt-rss rows lost the freshness line that's there to catch dead feeds. Fixed.
  • TalkBack, Boox: about 67 swipe stops; "News 10" heard as an unread count; full refresh on every fold. Fixed: tt-rss folded per section, headings read "News, 3 sources and 9 tt-rss feeds, folded".
  • Verdict: drop B as the structure; keep "Sources reads like the contents" as a view on top of A, and the time-per-section idea as its own proposal. Done: B v2 is that view; the idea is D.
Cycles 9–11 · v2

C. Adding a feed to tt-rss too

Built on A. With a tt-rss account connected, adding one feed asks whether it's just for the paper (the default) or in your tt-rss too. Round 1 cut v1 down to this one choice; Move to tt-rss waits.

What changes

  • Already in your tt-rss? The app checks first and opens that feed instead of asking.
  • "Just for the paper" is preselected, every time for now. A remembered answer can come later, if most adds turn out to go to tt-rss.
  • The category starts on the paper's own ("Articles from"). Picking another warns: "won't be in your paper".
  • Its back issues: a new tt-rss subscription arrives with ~20 old posts unread. "Mark its older posts read in tt-rss" is ticked by default, so the laptop count doesn't jump.
  • After adding: a sync runs at once, and the message has Undo (the app unsubscribes the feed it just added).
  • Slow server: stepped text while tt-rss downloads the feed. A timeout is "not sure yet", checked at the next sync, not a failure that invites a duplicate.
  • Untouched: onboarding, starter packs and OPML stay phone-only, except they skip feeds already in tt-rss.

For

  • One subscription list across your tt-rss apps, when you want it.
  • Explicit, as NetNewsWire does it; the default keeps today's behaviour.
  • Undo, so a wrong tap isn't a laptop chore.

Against

  • tt-rss strips some content (footnotes, Substack Notes): a phone feed can be better.
  • A site may block the server but not the phone.
  • More reliance on a project now living on a fork.

Round 1 · cycle 10 · personas

  • Owner on one category: a feed added to another category never reaches the paper. Fixed: category starts on the paper's, with a warning.
  • Owner: subscribing dumps the feed's backlog unread into the laptop counts. Fixed: "Mark its older posts read in tt-rss", on by default.
  • Owner, self-hoster: a remembered "tt-rss too" plus no way to unsubscribe makes a wrong tap a laptop chore. Fixed: phone is the default; Undo unsubscribes.
  • OPML, starter packs: importing your own tt-rss OPML makes 58 duplicates. Fixed: imports skip feeds already in tt-rss; they never ask.
  • Slow server: a timeout then "Add to this phone" makes a duplicate once the server finishes. Fixed: a timeout is "not sure yet", checked at the next sync.
  • Owner: Move to tt-rss can orphan settings if tt-rss stores a different address, or drop stars before tt-rss has fetched anything. Reduced: Move is deferred.
  • Self-hoster: Substack through tt-rss loses Notes and footnotes. Fixed: the dialog says so for known hosts and keeps the phone preselected.
New in round 1 · v1

D. Balance the paper by section

Both A's and B's critics found the same gap: with 54 feeds and seven slots, per-feed controls don't shape the paper. What the owner wants is "thoughtful, varied, some fun": a share of the 30 minutes per section. Sections come from tt-rss categories, OPML folders, or ones you make, and it works the same for every kind of source.

What changes

  • Settings › Sections and balance: each section with about how many minutes it gets. Off, the paper takes turns between sources as today.
  • A preview of tomorrow's contents, so the effect of a change is visible before the morning.
  • Order of sections is the order of the contents.
  • Within a section, turns between its sources work as today; stars still go first.

For

  • Shapes the paper at the level the reader thinks in.
  • Not tt-rss-only: OPML folders get it too.
  • Inoreader's per-folder digests and KTool's per-feed magazines point the same way.

Against

  • A new screen and a new idea to explain.
  • Minutes are estimates; a long article can blow a section's share.
  • Needs sections to exist first (A's switch, or OPML).
